## Why this matters

Every formula you write for the rest of your life refers to cells by address. And one habit you form here — how you lay data out — decides whether pivot tables and lookups work smoothly later or fight you at every turn.

## How it works

Every box is a **cell**, and every cell's address is its **column letter then row number**. Always that order.

`B7` = column B, row 7. Not `7B` — Excel rejects that.

Columns run A to Z, then AA, AB, all the way to XFD (16,384 of them). Rows run 1 to 1,048,576.

A **range** uses a colon:

| Notation | Means | Cells |
|---|---|---|
| `A1` | One cell | 1 |
| `A1:A10` | Ten cells down a column | 10 |
| `A1:D1` | Four cells across a row | 4 |
| `A1:D10` | A rectangle, 4 × 10 | 40 |
| `A1:A5,C1:C5` | Two separate blocks | 10 |
| `A:A` | The entire column | 1,048,576 |

The colon means "through". A comma means "and these too".

### The structure rule that matters most

**A row is a record. A column is a field.**

One employee per row. Name, Department, Salary each in their own column. Keep that discipline and Modules 6 to 8 work. Break it and they won't.

Two specific things to never do:

**Don't put two pieces of information in one cell.** `Anil Kumar (Sales)` can't be filtered by department. Two columns.

**Don't merge cells.** Merge looks tidy and breaks sorting, filtering, pivot tables and keyboard navigation — because merging keeps the value in the top-left cell and genuinely empties the rest. If you want a title centred across columns, use **Format Cells → Alignment → Center Across Selection**: visually identical, nothing breaks.

<Callout type="warning">
Whole-column references like `=SUM(E:E)` work, but they ask Excel to consider a million cells. Fine once; noticeably slow with several. Prefer an explicit range, or better, convert to a Table and use `Table1[CTC]` (Lesson 6.1).
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
Say ranges out loud as you write them and the notation stops feeling abstract. `A1:D10` is "A-one through D-ten" — a rectangle. `A2:A6,D2:D6` is "A-two through A-six, **and** D-two through D-six" — two blocks. The colon is "through"; the comma is "and also".
</Callout>

## Do it

| | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | EmpID | Name | Department | DOJ | CTC |
| **2** | E1001 | Anil Kumar | Sales | 12-Mar-2021 | 640000 |
| **3** | E1002 | Meera Nair | HR | 05-Jul-2022 | 520000 |
| **4** | E1003 | Rahul Shah | Sales | 19-Jan-2020 | 810000 |
| **5** | E1004 | Sneha Rao | Finance | 28-Sep-2023 | 470000 |

1. Type this in starting at A1.
2. Click **C3**. The Name Box reads `C3`; the formula bar reads `HR`.
3. Click **A2**, hold the mouse and drag to **E5**. While dragging, the Name Box shows `4R x 5C` — four rows by five columns.

<Shot id="1.3.2" />

4. Click the **column D header letter**. The whole column selects.
5. Click the **row 3 header number**. The whole row selects.
6. Click **A1**, then hold <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd> and click **C1** and **E1**. Three non-adjacent cells — the `A1,C1,E1` pattern.
7. Click **A1** and press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>End</Kbd>. The whole dataset selects in one keystroke. **This is how you select real data — never by dragging.**

<Shot id="1.3.3" />

### See why merge breaks things

8. Select **A1:E1** and click **Merge & Center**. It looks like a title bar.
9. Now try to sort the data — click one cell inside it and use Data → Sort. Excel refuses: *"To do this, all the merged cells need to be the same size."*
10. Unmerge. Instead select A1:E1, press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>1</Kbd> → Alignment → Horizontal → **Center Across Selection**.
11. Click **C1**. The Name Box says `C1` and the cell is still usable — but the title still appears centred. Same look, nothing broken.

## Shortcuts

<ShortcutTable>

| Shortcut | What it does | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `End` | Selects from here to the last used cell | Selecting an entire dataset instantly, before sorting or making a Table | Click A1, press it |
| `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `↓` | Extends the selection to the last filled cell below | Selecting one full column of data | Click E2, press it — note it stops at gaps |
| `Ctrl` + `Space` | Selects the whole column | Formatting an entire column at once | Then Ctrl+Shift+1 to format as numbers |
| `Shift` + `Space` | Selects the whole row | Deleting or highlighting one record | Then Ctrl+- to delete it |

</ShortcutTable>

## Watch out

<MistakeTable>

| Mistake | What you see | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing `7B` instead of `B7` | `#NAME?`, or treated as text | Column letter first, always |
| **Merging cells** | Sorting refuses; filters and pivots return blanks | Center Across Selection |
| Two facts in one cell | Can't filter by department or sort by surname | One piece of information per cell |
| Blank rows inside data | `Ctrl+Shift+↓` stops early; pivots read part of the data | Never a blank row inside a dataset |
| Two stacked header rows | Pivot tables can't identify the header | One header row, directly above the data |
| Drag-selecting 2,000 rows | Slow, and easy to miss the last row | `Ctrl+Shift+End` |

</MistakeTable>

<Callout type="story">
**Kavya, Recruitment Coordinator.** Her candidate tracker merged the role name across four rows to "group" candidates visually — Java Developer merged over rows 4–7, QA Engineer over 8–11.

It looked tidy. Then her manager asked for candidates by role and by source. The pivot table returned mostly blanks, because merged cells keep the value only in the top-left cell — the other three are genuinely empty.

The fix took ten minutes: unmerge everything, fill the role down into every row, rebuild the pivot. It then worked immediately.

**Formatting that makes data look organised often makes it functionally broken.** Keep the data flat and plain; make it look good with conditional formatting and charts instead.
</Callout>

## Practice

<Exercise id="1.3.e1">

<Scenario>
Document the structure of an attendance sheet so a colleague can write formulas against it.
</Scenario>

<Dataset name="Attendance_Register">

| | A | B | C | D | E | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | EmpID | Name | Date | Status | In Time | Out Time |
| **2** | E1001 | Anil | 01-Sep-2026 | Present | 09:12 | 18:05 |
| **3** | E1002 | Meera | 01-Sep-2026 | Present | 09:45 | 18:20 |
| **4** | E1003 | Rahul | 01-Sep-2026 | Absent | | |
| **5** | E1004 | Sneha | 01-Sep-2026 | Present | 08:58 | 17:40 |
| **6** | E1005 | Kabir | 01-Sep-2026 | Half Day | 09:05 | 13:30 |

</Dataset>

<Task>

Write the correct range notation for each:

1. The header row only
2. All the Status values, excluding the header
3. The whole table including headers
4. Rahul's entire record
5. The EmpID and Status columns together, data only
6. The single cell holding Kabir's out time

Then:

7. Click A1 and press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>End</Kbd>. What range selects? Why does it include E4 and F4, which are empty?
8. Click E2 and press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>↓</Kbd>. Where does it stop, and why is that a warning rather than a bug?

</Task>

<Expected>
1. `A1:F1` · 2. `D2:D6` · 3. `A1:F6` · 4. `A4:F4` · 5. `A2:A6,D2:D6` · 6. `F6` · 7. `A1:F6` · 8. stops at E3, because E4 is blank
</Expected>

<Solution>

```
1. A1:F1
2. D2:D6
3. A1:F6
4. A4:F4
5. A2:A6,D2:D6
6. F6
```

</Solution>

<Why>

**Step 7's answer explains a rule you'll rely on constantly.** Excel's used range is always a *rectangle*, never a jagged shape — so E4 and F4 are included even though Rahul was absent and they're empty. That's why `=COUNT(E2:E6)` returns 4 rather than 5: the cells are in the range, they just hold nothing.

**Step 8 is the more important one.** `Ctrl+Shift+↓` stops at the first blank, so it selects only E2:E3 — and if you built a formula on that selection you'd silently exclude three records. That behaviour is genuinely useful as a **gap detector**: run it down each column of a new dataset and if the columns stop at different rows, you've found missing data before it corrupts anything.

</Why>

</Exercise>
