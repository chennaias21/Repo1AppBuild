## Why this matters

Tenure, age, notice periods, probation end dates, invoice ageing, time-to-hire, working days in a month — every HR and finance report runs on date arithmetic. And because dates are secretly numbers (Lesson 2.2), most of it is simpler than it looks.

## How it works

Since a date is a serial number, plain arithmetic works:

<FormulaBlock>
```
=TODAY()-D2        days between the joining date and today
=D2+90             90 days after the joining date — probation end
=D2-C2             days between two dates
```
</FormulaBlock>

<Callout type="warning">
Subtracting two dates often auto-formats the result as a **date** — so you see `04-Sep-1914` instead of `2006`. Press `Ctrl+Shift+~` (General format) to see the actual number. This catches out almost everyone the first time.
</Callout>

### DATEDIF — tenure in the units people ask for

<FormulaBlock>
```
=DATEDIF(D2, TODAY(), "y")     complete years         → 4
=DATEDIF(D2, TODAY(), "m")     complete months        → 54
=DATEDIF(D2, TODAY(), "ym")    months past the year   → 6
=DATEDIF(D2, TODAY(), "d")     days                   → 1643
```
</FormulaBlock>

Combine them for the format HR actually uses:

<FormulaBlock>
```
=DATEDIF(D2,TODAY(),"y")&"y "&DATEDIF(D2,TODAY(),"ym")&"m"     →  "4y 6m"
```
</FormulaBlock>

<Shot id="4.7.1" />

<Callout type="tip">
`DATEDIF` is a hidden function — Excel offers no autocomplete and no tooltip for it, and it isn't in the function library. It works in every version anyway. Type it out in full; don't wait for Excel to suggest it.
</Callout>

### Month-end and month-shift

<FormulaBlock>
```
=EOMONTH(TODAY(),0)      last day of this month
=EOMONTH(TODAY(),-1)     last day of last month
=EOMONTH(D2,0)+1         first day of the following month
=EDATE(D2,6)             same date, 6 months later
```
</FormulaBlock>

`EOMONTH` handles February, leap years and 30 vs 31 days for you. Any formula that adds 30 to get "next month" is wrong twelve times a year.

<Shot id="4.7.2" />

### Working days

<FormulaBlock>
```
=NETWORKDAYS(C2, D2)              working days between two dates, inclusive
=NETWORKDAYS(C2, D2, $H$2:$H$12)  excluding a holiday list
=WORKDAY(C2, 15, $H$2:$H$12)      the date 15 working days after C2
```
</FormulaBlock>

Both skip Saturdays and Sundays by default. `NETWORKDAYS.INTL` lets you define a different weekend — essential if your business runs a six-day week.

The holiday range is just a column of dates somewhere on the sheet. **Maintain it once a year and every date calculation in the workbook becomes accurate.**

<Shot id="4.7.3" />

### Pulling parts out

<FormulaBlock>
```
=YEAR(D2)      2021
=MONTH(D2)     3
=TEXT(D2,"mmm")   "Mar"      — for grouping and charts
=WEEKDAY(D2,2)    1 = Monday … 7 = Sunday
```
</FormulaBlock>

## Do it

An HR master with joining dates.

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Employee | DOJ | DOB |
| **2** | Anil Kumar | 12-Mar-2021 | 04-Aug-1994 |
| **3** | Meera Nair | 05-Jul-2022 | 19-Feb-1990 |
| **4** | Rahul Shah | 19-Jan-2020 | 27-Nov-1986 |

1. **D2** — tenure in years: `=DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")`
2. **E2** — tenure formatted: `=DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")&"y "&DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"ym")&"m"`
3. **F2** — age: `=DATEDIF(C2,TODAY(),"y")`
4. **G2** — probation end, 90 days: `=B2+90`. Format as date.
5. **H2** — confirmation due at the end of the probation month: `=EOMONTH(B2+90,0)`

<Shot id="4.7.4" />

6. Build a holiday list in `K2:K12` — a column of dates.
7. **I2** — working days served this month: `=NETWORKDAYS(EOMONTH(TODAY(),-1)+1,TODAY(),$K$2:$K$12)`
8. **J2** — the review date, 20 working days out: `=WORKDAY(TODAY(),20,$K$2:$K$12)`

### The live-vs-static trap

9. In L2 press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>;</Kbd> — a static date is typed in.
10. In M2 type `=TODAY()`. Both look identical today.
11. **They are completely different.** `=TODAY()` recalculates every time the file opens. If you'd used it to stamp *when a record was created*, every historic record would show today's date the moment anyone opened the workbook — and the original dates would be gone with no way to recover them.

<Shot id="4.7.5" />

Use `=TODAY()` for "how old is this *now*". Use `Ctrl+;` for "this happened on".

## Shortcuts

<ShortcutTable>

| Shortcut | What it does | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Ctrl` + `;` | Inserts today's date as a **static** value | Recording when something actually happened — a date of receipt, an approval | Stamping a claim as approved today |
| `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `~` | General format — strips date formatting | Seeing why a date subtraction shows `04-Sep-1914` instead of `2006` | Diagnosing any date result |
| `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `3` | Date format | Turning a serial number back into a readable date | After `DATEVALUE` or a date calculation |

</ShortcutTable>

## Watch out

<MistakeTable>

| Mistake | What you see | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Date subtraction auto-formatted as a date** | `04-Sep-1914` instead of `2006` | `Ctrl+Shift+~` to set General format |
| Using `=TODAY()` to stamp a historic event | Every past record shows today's date, permanently | `Ctrl+;` for static stamps |
| Adding 30 for "next month" | Wrong in every month that isn't 30 days | `EOMONTH` or `EDATE` |
| `DATEDIF` with the dates the wrong way round | `#NUM!` | Earlier date first, always |
| Text dates from a system export | `#VALUE!` on any date arithmetic | Check alignment; `DATEVALUE` or Text to Columns (Lesson 2.2) |
| `NETWORKDAYS` without a holiday list | Public holidays counted as working days | Maintain a holiday range and reference it |
| Assuming `NETWORKDAYS` knows your weekend | Sat/Sun assumed — wrong for a six-day week | `NETWORKDAYS.INTL` with a weekend code |
| Expecting `DATEDIF` in autocomplete | You conclude it doesn't exist | It's undocumented but works. Type it in full. |

</MistakeTable>

<Callout type="story">
**Deepak, HR Executive.** His gratuity eligibility report used `=(TODAY()-B2)/365` to get years of service, then rounded down. Anyone at 5 years or more was flagged eligible.

Two employees at 4 years and 363 days showed as 5.00 years after rounding, because 365 ignores leap days and the accumulated drift pushed them over. Both were flagged eligible. One received a provisional gratuity calculation in writing before the error was caught, and it had to be withdrawn.

`=DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")` returns **complete** years and handles leap years correctly. It would have returned 4 for both. **Dividing by 365 is never right for tenure** — the drift is small, but eligibility thresholds are exact, and a threshold is precisely where a small error becomes a real consequence.
</Callout>

## Practice

<Exercise id="4.7.e1">

<Scenario>
An HR master needing tenure, probation dates, retirement projections and a working-days calculation for the current month.
</Scenario>

<Dataset name="HR_Employee_Master" starter="starter/04-07-dates.xlsx">

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Employee | DOJ | DOB |
| **2** | Anil Kumar | 12-Mar-2021 | 04-Aug-1994 |
| **3** | Meera Nair | 05-Jul-2022 | 19-Feb-1990 |
| **4** | Rahul Shah | 19-Jan-2020 | 27-Nov-1986 |
| **5** | Sneha Rao | 28-Sep-2025 | 11-Jun-1998 |
| **6** | Kabir Menon | 03-Feb-2019 | 22-Dec-1982 |

Holiday list in **M2:M6**: `26-Jan-2026`, `15-Aug-2026`, `02-Oct-2026`, `25-Dec-2026`, `01-Jan-2027`

</Dataset>

<Task>

1. **D2** — complete years of service.
2. **E2** — service as `4y 6m`.
3. **F2** — age in complete years.
4. **G2** — probation end date, 90 calendar days from DOJ.
5. **H2** — confirmation due date: the last day of the month in which probation ends.
6. **I2** — `Eligible` if service is 5 complete years or more, else `Not yet`. Use `DATEDIF`, not division.
7. **J2** — working days from the start of the current month to today, excluding the holiday list.
8. **K2** — retirement date, assuming age 58: the last day of the month in which they turn 58.

</Task>

<Expected>
Row 2 (DOJ 12-Mar-2021): D2 = 5, E2 = `5y 6m`, G2 = 10-Jun-2021, H2 = 30-Jun-2021 · Row 5 (DOJ 28-Sep-2025): D5 = 1, I5 `Not yet` · Row 6 (DOJ 03-Feb-2019): I6 `Eligible`
</Expected>

<Hint>
For step 8, add 58 years to the date of birth with `EDATE(C2,58*12)`, then wrap it in `EOMONTH(...,0)`.
</Hint>

<Solution>

```
D2: =DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")
E2: =DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")&"y "&DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"ym")&"m"
F2: =DATEDIF(C2,TODAY(),"y")
G2: =B2+90
H2: =EOMONTH(B2+90,0)
I2: =IF(DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")>=5,"Eligible","Not yet")
J2: =NETWORKDAYS(EOMONTH(TODAY(),-1)+1,TODAY(),$M$2:$M$6)
K2: =EOMONTH(EDATE(C2,58*12),0)
```

</Solution>

<Why>

**Step 6 is the one that matters.** `=DATEDIF(B2,TODAY(),"y")` returns complete years and handles leap years exactly. The tempting alternative, `=ROUNDDOWN((TODAY()-B2)/365,0)`, drifts by about a day every four years — so someone at 4 years and 363 days can calculate as 5. On a threshold that triggers a statutory payment, that drift is the difference between correct and legally wrong.

**Step 7's structure is worth keeping.** `EOMONTH(TODAY(),-1)+1` means "the day after the end of last month", which is the first day of this month — and it stays correct in February, in leap years, and across a year boundary. Anyone building this with `DATE(YEAR(TODAY()),MONTH(TODAY()),1)` gets the same answer with more moving parts, and anyone using `TODAY()-30` gets it wrong most of the year.

**Step 8 composes two functions and shows why `EDATE` exists.** `EDATE(C2,58*12)` lands on the same day of the month, 58 years on — handling the 29-February edge case that adding `58*365` would get wrong. Wrapping it in `EOMONTH(...,0)` moves it to month-end, which is how most retirement policies are actually written.

**One thing to notice across the whole exercise:** every result that uses `TODAY()` changes tomorrow. That's correct for tenure and age — they genuinely are one day older. It would be badly wrong for a "date of joining" or "date approved" field, which must be a static stamp. The same function is right in one column and destructive in the next.

</Why>

</Exercise>
