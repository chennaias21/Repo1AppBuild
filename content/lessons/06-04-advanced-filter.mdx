## Why this matters

AutoFilter hides rows in place. Sometimes you need the matching records *somewhere else* — a separate sheet to send to a regional manager, a clean extract for a mail merge, or a unique list of departments to build a dropdown from. That's Advanced Filter, and it does two things AutoFilter can't: OR conditions across different columns, and extraction to a new location.

## How it works

Advanced Filter needs a **criteria range** — a small block on the sheet with headers matching your data, and the conditions written underneath.

**The layout rule is the whole trick:**

<FormulaBlock>
```
Conditions on the SAME row      = AND
Conditions on DIFFERENT rows    = OR
```
</FormulaBlock>

So this criteria range means *"North AND more than 20 units"*:

| Region | Units |
|---|---|
| North | >20 |

And this means *"North OR South"*, regardless of units:

| Region | Units |
|---|---|
| North | |
| South | |

And this means *"North with over 20 units, OR South with any units"*:

| Region | Units |
|---|---|
| North | >20 |
| South | |

<Shot id="6.4.1" />

That last pattern is what AutoFilter genuinely cannot do. AutoFilter combines columns with AND only — you can't ask for "Sales team earning over 700k, or anyone in Ops regardless of salary" in one pass.

<Callout type="warning">
The criteria headers must **match your data headers exactly** — same spelling, no trailing spaces. A mismatch doesn't error; the filter just silently ignores that condition and returns more rows than you expected. Copy the headers rather than retyping them.
</Callout>

### Running it

**Data → Advanced** (or <Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>A</Kbd>+<Kbd>Q</Kbd>) and fill three boxes:

| Box | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| List range | Your data, including headers |
| Criteria range | The criteria block, including its headers |
| Copy to | Where the extract should land — only enabled if you chose "Copy to another location" |

Tick **Unique records only** to deduplicate the output.

<Shot id="6.4.2" />

### Extracting a unique list

Point the list range at one column, leave the criteria range empty, tick **Unique records only**, and copy to a blank cell. You get every distinct value once — which is exactly what you need to build a data validation dropdown (Lesson 6.7).

<Callout type="tip">
On Microsoft 365, `=UNIQUE(Orders[Region])` does this in one formula and updates live. `=FILTER()` handles the conditional extraction. Advanced Filter still matters because it works in every version, and because a criteria range is something a non-technical colleague can edit without touching a formula.
</Callout>

### Its one real limitation

Advanced Filter output is a **snapshot**. Change the source data and the extract doesn't update — you re-run the filter. `FILTER()` and `UNIQUE()` are live. If you're on 365 and the extract needs to stay current, use those instead.

## Do it

| | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | OrderID | Region | Product | Units |
| **2** | SO1001 | North | Laptop | 12 |
| **3** | SO1002 | South | Monitor | 30 |
| **4** | SO1003 | North | Laptop | 8 |
| **5** | SO1004 | East | Keyboard | 45 |
| **6** | SO1005 | West | Monitor | 22 |
| **7** | SO1006 | North | Keyboard | 60 |

### An AND condition

1. **Copy** A1:D1 to **F1:I1** — copy, don't retype.
2. In **G2** type `North`. In **I2** type `>20`.
3. <Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>A</Kbd>+<Kbd>Q</Kbd>. Choose **Copy to another location**.
4. List range: `$A$1:$D$7` · Criteria range: `$F$1:$I$2` · Copy to: `$F$6`
5. OK. You get SO1006 only — North *and* over 20 units.

### An OR condition

6. Clear the extract. In **G3** type `South` and leave I3 empty.
7. Criteria range is now `$F$1:$I$3` — two condition rows.
8. Re-run. You get SO1006 (North, >20) **and** both South orders, whatever their units.

### Unique list

9. In **K1** type `Region` — matching the data header exactly.
10. <Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>A</Kbd>+<Kbd>Q</Kbd> → Copy to another location.
11. List range: `$B$1:$B$7` · Criteria range: **leave empty** · Copy to: `$K$3` · tick **Unique records only**.
12. You get North, South, East, West — once each.

<Shot id="6.4.4" />

13. On 365, compare: `=UNIQUE(B2:B7)` in one cell gives the same result and stays current.

## Shortcuts

<ShortcutTable>

| Shortcut | What it does | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Alt` + `A` + `Q` | Opens the Advanced Filter dialog | OR conditions across columns, or extracting to a new location | After building the criteria range |
| `Ctrl` + `Shift` + `L` | Toggles AutoFilter | Checking a simple condition before building a criteria range — often AutoFilter is enough | Quick single-column check |
| `F5` then the range | Jumps to and selects a named range | Re-selecting your criteria block on a crowded sheet | Type `$F$1:$I$3` into the Name Box |

</ShortcutTable>

## Watch out

<MistakeTable>

| Mistake | What you see | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Criteria header doesn't match the data header** | Condition silently ignored, too many rows returned | Copy the headers, never retype them |
| Blank row inside the criteria range | Matches everything — the blank row is "no condition" | Keep criteria rows contiguous |
| Criteria range selection excludes the headers | The first condition is read as a header | Always include the header row |
| Expecting the extract to update | It's a snapshot | Re-run, or use `FILTER()` on 365 |
| `>20` typed in a text-formatted cell | Treated as literal text, matches nothing | Ensure the criteria cell is General format |
| Copy-to destination has existing data | Excel overwrites it without asking | Extract to an empty area |
| Using it for a simple single-column filter | Slower than AutoFilter for no benefit | AutoFilter unless you need OR or extraction |

</MistakeTable>

<Callout type="story">
**Naveen, HR Shared Services.** Every month he sent each of four regional managers a list of their own employees. His method: filter the master to one region, select the visible rows, copy, paste into a new workbook, save, email. Four times. About forty minutes, and twice he pasted the wrong region because he'd forgotten to clear the previous filter.

He rebuilt it with Advanced Filter: four criteria cells on a control sheet, four extract areas, and a recorded macro (Lesson 10.6) that re-ran all four. The monthly task became one button and two minutes.

The detail that made it reliable rather than just faster: **he copied the header text into the criteria range instead of typing it.** His first attempt had typed `Region ` with a trailing space, and the filter had silently ignored that condition and returned all 240 employees to every regional file. That would have been a genuine data-sharing problem, and nothing about the output looked wrong.
</Callout>

## Practice

<Exercise id="6.4.e1">

<Scenario>
Extract three different employee lists from a master file, including one that AutoFilter cannot produce, plus a unique department list for a dropdown.
</Scenario>

<Dataset name="HR_Employee_Master" starter="starter/06-04-advanced-filter.xlsx">

| | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | EmpID | Name | Dept | CTC |
| **2** | E1001 | Anil Kumar | Sales | 640000 |
| **3** | E1002 | Meera Nair | HR | 520000 |
| **4** | E1003 | Rahul Shah | Sales | 810000 |
| **5** | E1004 | Sneha Rao | Finance | 470000 |
| **6** | E1005 | Kabir Menon | Ops | 920000 |
| **7** | E1006 | Divya Iyer | Sales | 725000 |
| **8** | E1007 | Arjun Das | HR | 580000 |
| **9** | E1008 | Nisha Roy | Ops | 690000 |

</Dataset>

<Task>

1. Build a criteria range by **copying** A1:D1 to F1:I1.
2. Extract everyone in Sales earning more than 700,000. How many rows?
3. Now extract **Sales earning over 700,000, OR anyone in Ops regardless of salary.** Could AutoFilter do this in one pass?
4. Extract a unique list of departments to a blank area, for use as a dropdown source later.
5. Deliberately break it: change your criteria header from `Dept` to `Dept ` with a trailing space and re-run. How many rows come back, and does anything warn you?
6. Fix the header. Then extract everyone earning between 500,000 and 750,000 — this needs two conditions on the same column.
7. On Microsoft 365, reproduce step 3 with a single `FILTER` formula.
8. Add a new employee to the source data. Does your step 2 extract update?

</Task>

<Expected>
Step 2: 2 rows (Rahul, Divya) · Step 3: 4 rows — no, AutoFilter combines columns with AND only · Step 4: Sales, HR, Finance, Ops · Step 5: all 8 rows, with no warning · Step 6: 3 rows (Meera 520,000, Arjun 580,000, Nisha 690,000) · Step 8: no — the extract is a snapshot
</Expected>

<Hint>
For step 6, two conditions on the same column need the header twice, side by side, with one condition under each.
</Hint>

<Solution>

```
Step 2 criteria:          Dept    CTC
                          Sales   >700000

Step 3 criteria:          Dept    CTC
                          Sales   >700000
                          Ops

Step 4: List range $C$1:$C$9 · Criteria range empty · tick Unique records only

Step 6 criteria:          CTC        CTC
                          >=500000   <=750000

Step 7: =FILTER(A2:D9,((C2:C9="Sales")*(D2:D9>700000))+(C2:C9="Ops"),"None")
```

</Solution>

<Why>

**Step 3 is why Advanced Filter still exists.** AutoFilter joins columns with AND, so "Sales over 700k" and "all of Ops" cannot be asked together — you'd have to run two filters and combine the results by hand. A second criteria row expresses it directly, because different rows mean OR.

**Step 5 is the failure mode worth remembering.** A trailing space in the criteria header makes Excel unable to match it to a data column, so it treats that condition as absent. With both conditions ignored, every row matches. Nothing errors, nothing warns, and you get a full extract that looks like a successful filter — which, in Naveen's case, would have meant sending every region the whole company's data. Copy headers; never retype them.

**Step 6 shows the same-column pattern.** Excel allows a header to appear twice in a criteria range, and conditions on the same row are ANDed — so `>=500000` under one `CTC` and `<=750000` under the other gives a between. It looks odd and it's the correct construction.

**Step 8 is the limitation to keep in mind.** Advanced Filter writes a snapshot. `FILTER()` from step 7 is live, so on Microsoft 365 that's the better tool for anything that needs to stay current. Advanced Filter earns its place in older versions, and when a colleague needs to change the criteria without touching a formula.

</Why>

</Exercise>
