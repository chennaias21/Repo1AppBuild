## Why this matters

Sooner or later you'll inherit a workbook that produces a number nobody can explain, or your own model will return something that's clearly wrong. These five tools turn a twenty-minute hunt into a two-minute check — and the first one finds the most common cause of a silently broken model: a cell where someone typed a value over your formula.

## How it works

### Show Formulas — <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>`</Kbd>

Displays every formula on the sheet instead of its result. Press again to return.

This is the fastest way to review a workbook you didn't build. **What you're looking for: a cell in a formula column that has no `=`.** That's where someone "corrected" a figure by hand, and it has never updated since.

<Shot id="3.7.1" />

### Trace Precedents and Dependents

**Formulas → Trace Precedents** draws arrows *to* the cells feeding the selected cell. **Trace Dependents** draws arrows *to* the cells that depend on it.

Precedents answer "where does this number come from?" Dependents answer "what breaks if I change this?" — which is the question to ask before editing anything in a model you don't fully understand.

`Ctrl` + `[` jumps straight to the precedents; `Ctrl` + `]` to the dependents. **Remove Arrows** clears them.

<Shot id="3.7.2" />

### Evaluate Formula

**Formulas → Evaluate Formula** steps through a calculation one operation at a time. Click **Evaluate** repeatedly and watch each piece resolve until the error appears.

Slower than `F9` but it shows the *whole* sequence, which is what you want on a deeply nested formula where you don't yet know which part is wrong.

### F9 on a fragment

Press <Kbd>F2</Kbd> to edit, select just one part of the formula with the mouse, press <Kbd>F9</Kbd>. Excel replaces that fragment with its result.

**Press <Kbd>Esc</Kbd>, not Enter** — Enter would commit the evaluated fragment as a hard-coded value, permanently replacing your formula.

<Callout type="warning">
That Esc-versus-Enter distinction matters. Pressing Enter after an `F9` bakes the current result into the formula as a literal number. The cell then shows the right answer today and never updates again — exactly the silent staleness this lesson exists to find.
</Callout>

### Go To Special — the systematic sweep

<Kbd>F5</Kbd> → **Special** selects cells by type:

| Option | Finds |
|---|---|
| **Formulas → Errors** | Every error cell at once, with a count in the status bar |
| **Constants** | Typed values — in a formula column, these are your bugs |
| **Formulas** | Every formula cell, for protecting or colouring |
| **Row differences** | The one cell that breaks a pattern |

<Callout type="tip">
`F5` → Special → **Constants** on a column that should be entirely formulas is the single most valuable audit in Excel. Whatever it selects is a cell someone typed over — the commonest way a working model quietly becomes wrong.
</Callout>

## Do it

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Employee | Basic | PF |
| **2** | Anil Kumar | 45000 | `=B2*0.12` |
| **3** | Meera Nair | 62000 | `=B3*0.12` |
| **4** | Rahul Shah | 38000 | **`4560`** |
| **5** | Sneha Rao | 51000 | `=B5*0.12` |

C4 is a typed number — someone "fixed" it.

1. In C7: `=SUM(C2:C5)`. Everything looks fine.
2. Press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>`</Kbd>. **C4 stands out immediately** — it's the only cell in the column without an `=`.
3. Press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>`</Kbd> again to return.

<Shot id="3.7.3" />

4. Now find it systematically: select C2:C5, press <Kbd>F5</Kbd> → **Special** → **Constants** → OK. **C4 alone is selected.**
5. Change B4 to `50000`. C4 still shows 4,560 — it will never update.
6. Restore the formula: `=B4*0.12`.

### Trace where a number comes from

7. Click C7 → **Formulas → Trace Precedents.** Arrows point to C2:C5.
8. Click B2 → **Trace Dependents.** Arrows show what depends on it.
9. **Formulas → Remove Arrows** to clear.

<Shot id="3.7.4" />

### Step through a formula

10. In E2 build something deliberately awkward: `=IFERROR(B2/(C2-C2),"Div zero")`
11. **Formulas → Evaluate Formula.** Click **Evaluate** repeatedly — watch `C2-C2` become `0`, then the division error appear, then `IFERROR` catch it.
12. Now try `F9`: press <Kbd>F2</Kbd>, select `(C2-C2)`, press <Kbd>F9</Kbd> → it shows `0`. Press <Kbd>Esc</Kbd>.
13. Do it again but press **Enter** instead. The formula now contains a literal `0` where the calculation was. Press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>Z</Kbd> — and note that was a genuine, permanent change.

### Find every error at once

14. Create two errors somewhere — `=1/0` and `=SUMM(1,2)`.
15. Press <Kbd>F5</Kbd> → Special → **Formulas** → untick everything except **Errors** → OK. Both select; read the count in the status bar.

<Shot id="3.7.5" />

## Shortcuts

<ShortcutTable>

| Shortcut | What it does | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Ctrl` + `` ` `` | Shows every formula instead of results | **First action on any inherited workbook.** The cell with no `=` is your bug. | Press again to return |
| `F5` → Special → Constants | Selects every typed value | Auditing a formula column for cells someone overwrote | Select the column first |
| `Ctrl` + `[` | Jumps to the cells a formula references | Tracing where a number comes from, fast | `Ctrl` + `]` for dependents |
| `F9` on a fragment | Evaluates that piece only | Pinpointing which part of a formula is wrong | **Press Esc, never Enter** |

</ShortcutTable>

## Watch out

<MistakeTable>

| Mistake | What you see | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressing Enter after `F9`** | The fragment is baked in as a literal value, permanently | Always `Esc` |
| Assuming a formula column is all formulas | One typed cell never updates, silently | `F5` → Special → Constants |
| Wrapping an error in `IFERROR` before diagnosing | The symptom is hidden; the cause isn't fixed | `F5` → Special → Errors first, and read the count |
| Editing a model without checking dependents | You break three downstream calculations | `Ctrl` + `]` before changing anything |
| Trusting a named range because the formula reads correctly | The name may point at a deleted range | Name Manager (Lesson 3.6) |
| Leaving trace arrows on | The sheet becomes unreadable | Formulas → Remove Arrows |

</MistakeTable>

<Callout type="story">
**Sridhar, Payroll Executive.** His PF register had worked for eighteen months. Then an auditor queried one employee's deduction — it didn't match 12% of that person's basic.

Sridhar checked the formula. It was correct. He checked the rate cell. Correct. He recalculated. No change.

Eventually he pressed <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>`</Kbd>. One cell in a column of 340 had no `=` — a plain number. Someone had typed over the formula a year earlier to "correct" a figure during a salary revision, and it had paid that fixed amount every month since, regardless of the employee's actual salary.

**A typed value in a formula column is invisible in normal view and unmistakable in Show Formulas.** He now runs `F5` → Special → Constants on every formula column before each monthly filing. It takes ten seconds.
</Callout>

## Practice

<Exercise id="3.7.e1">

<Scenario>
Audit an inherited payroll sheet that produces a figure nobody can explain.
</Scenario>

<Dataset name="HR_Employee_Master" starter="starter/03-07-auditing.xlsx">

| | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Employee | Basic | PF | HRA |
| **2** | Anil Kumar | 45000 | `=B2*$G$1` | `=B2*$G$2` |
| **3** | Meera Nair | 62000 | `=B3*$G$1` | `=B3*$G$2` |
| **4** | Rahul Shah | 38000 | **`4560`** | `=B4*$G$2` |
| **5** | Sneha Rao | 51000 | `=B5*$G$1` | **`20000`** |
| **6** | Kabir Menon | 47000 | `=B6*$G$1` | `=B6*$G$2` |

**G1** = `12%` · **G2** = `40%`. C4 and D5 are typed values.

</Dataset>

<Task>

1. Total columns C and D. Do the figures look plausible?
2. Press <Kbd>Ctrl</Kbd>+<Kbd>`</Kbd>. How many cells lack an `=`, and which are they?
3. Find them systematically with `F5` → Special → Constants on C2:D6.
4. Change B4 to `50000` and B5 to `55000`. Which cells fail to update, and what does that tell you?
5. Restore both to proper formulas. Confirm they now respond.
6. Click the C total and use Trace Precedents. Then click G1 and use Trace Dependents — how many cells depend on the PF rate?
7. Build `=IFERROR(B2/(G1-G1),"Error caught")` somewhere and step through it with Evaluate Formula.
8. Use `F9` on the `(G1-G1)` fragment. Press **Esc**. Now repeat and press **Enter** instead — what changed, and why is that dangerous?
9. Create two errors on the sheet, then select all error cells in one action. What's the count?

</Task>

<Expected>
Step 2: two cells — C4 and D5 · Step 4: C4 and D5 don't change · Step 8: pressing Enter replaces the fragment with a literal `0`, permanently
</Expected>

<Solution>

```
Step 3: select C2:D6 → F5 → Special → Constants → OK
Step 5: C4: =B4*$G$1      D5: =B5*$G$2
Step 6: click C7 → Formulas → Trace Precedents
        click G1 → Formulas → Trace Dependents
Step 9: F5 → Special → Formulas → tick only Errors → OK
```

</Solution>

<Why>

**Step 1's answer is yes — and that's the problem.** The totals are plausible. Nothing errors, nothing looks odd, and C4's 4,560 is a perfectly reasonable PF figure for someone on 38,000. You would never find this by looking at the results.

**Step 4 is what makes it a live bug rather than a historical one.** Once B4 changes, C4 keeps paying the old amount forever. In Sridhar's case that ran for a year across a monthly statutory filing.

**Step 8's Esc-versus-Enter distinction is worth internalising.** `F9` is a diagnostic; Enter turns it into a permanent edit that replaces working logic with today's answer. The irony is neat: the tool for finding baked-in values will bake one in if you press the wrong key.

</Why>

</Exercise>
